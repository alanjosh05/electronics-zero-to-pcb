01 · the big idea

Every board you'll ever design comes down to three words.

You write apps. One day they'll talk to a board you designed, with an LED that blinks when your voice agent is listening, a button, a mic. Under every one of those parts, the same three things are happening:

  • Push: how hard electricity is shoved. Real name: voltage.
  • Flow: how much electricity moves. Real name: current.
  • Squeeze: how hard it is to get through. Real name: resistance.

Today's win: you'll light a real LED from your board, and you'll calculate which resistor to use instead of guessing.

Use ← → keys or the buttons below. ~15 minutes.

02 · the loop

Electricity only moves if it can get back home.

A toy train only runs if the track is a full circle. Pull out one piece of track and every train stops.

A circuit is that full circle: from the battery's + side, through your parts, back to its − side. A switch is just a drawbridge in the track. Click the switch in the diagram.

On diagrams we draw flow going from + round to −. This is called conventional current; every schematic you'll ever read uses it, so we will too.[SparkFun]

03 · push

Voltage is how hard the water is pushed.

Water from a tall tank on the roof sprays out harder than water from a bucket on the table. A taller tank pushes harder.

In the water picture, voltage is the pressure.[SparkFun] We measure it in volts (V). Things you already own:

  • 🔋 AA battery: 1.5 V
  • 🔌 USB port, and the Arduino Uno's "5V" pin: 5 V[Arduino]
  • 📶 ESP32 and most modern chips run on 3.3 V
  • 🟥 the square "PP3" battery: 9 V

Voltage on its own doesn't move anything. It's a push waiting for a path.

04 · flow

Current is how much water actually goes past.

Count the marbles rolling past your finger every second. Lots of marbles = big current. A few marbles = small current.

Current is the flow.[SparkFun] We measure it in amps (A). Small things use milliamps (mA): 1 A = 1000 mA.

  • 💡 a small indicator LED: about 10–20 mA[SparkFun]
  • 📱 a phone charging: a few amps, that's hundreds of times more

⚠️ This matters for your boards: one Arduino Uno pin must never go above 40 mA, and ~20 mA is the recommended level.[Arduino]

05 · squeeze + the one rule

Resistance squeezes the pipe. Ohm's law ties all three together.

Step on a garden hose. The tap pushes just as hard, but less water comes out. Your foot is the resistance.

Resistance is measured in ohms (Ω). A resistor is a tiny part whose only job is to be a foot on the hose. The rule linking push, flow and squeeze is Ohm's law:[SparkFun]

V = I × R  →  I = V ÷ R

(I is the letter for current.) Push harder → more flow. Squeeze harder → less flow. Play with the sliders and watch the dots.

Flow: 22.7 mA
06 · the greedy light

An LED is a one-way door that glows, and it's greedy.

An LED is like a kid at a juice tap. Give it a cup and it's happy. Give it the whole tap and it drinks until it pops.

  • One-way: current only goes in the long leg (anode, +) and out the short leg (cathode, −). Backwards = nothing lights.[SparkFun]
  • Uses up some push: a red LED "eats" about 2 V; blue/white ones about 3 V. This is its forward voltage (Vf).[LME]
  • Greedy: past that voltage, a tiny bit more push makes current shoot up, so the LED can't limit itself. It needs a resistor to be its cup.[LME]

With 220 Ω: (5 − 2) ÷ 220 ≈ 13.6 mA. A happy, glowing LED. ✅
07 · the recipe

Picking an LED resistor in 3 moves.

  1. Leftover push: supply − LED.  5 V − 2 V = 3 V left for the resistor.
  2. Divide by the flow you want: 3 V ÷ 0.015 A = 200 Ω.[LME]
  3. Round UP to a value you can buy. Resistors come in fixed sizes (10, 12, 15, 18, 22, 27, 33, 39, 47, 56, 68, 82, ×10, ×100…), the "E12" series.[EN] 200 → 220 Ω.
R = (Vsupply − VLED) ÷ I

Why round up? A bigger resistor means a little less current: a slightly dimmer LED, but never a cooked one. The resistor only turns ~0.05 W into heat here, so a normal ¼ W resistor is plenty.[LME]

09 · hands on

Now do it for real on your breadboard.

You need: Arduino (or any 5 V USB board), breadboard, 1 red LED, one 220 Ω or 330 Ω resistor, 2 wires.

No 220 Ω? 330 Ω is SparkFun's own default for this circuit, a bit dimmer and just as safe.[SparkFun] Resistor colour bands: red-red-brown = 220, orange-orange-brown = 330.

CARDResistor · Ω · the foot on the hose

Use when: you need to limit current (LEDs, protecting a microcontroller pin).

Pick it by: R = (Vsupply − Vpart) ÷ I, then round up to E12.

CARDLED · the greedy one-way light

Use when: you want a status light (power on, listening, error).

Rules: long leg → +, always with a resistor, ~5–20 mA, red ≈ 2 V, blue/white ≈ 3 V.
