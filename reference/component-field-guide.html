01

Resistor

· the foot on the hose

Use when: limiting current (LEDs, protecting pins), dividers, pull-ups.

Rules: R = (Vsupply − Vpart) ÷ I → round up to E12 (10 12 15 18 22 27 33 39 47 56 68 82) · power rating ≥ 2× heat · bands: red-red-brown 220 Ω, orange-orange-brown 330 Ω, brown-black-red 1 kΩ. [Electronics Notes]

→ lesson 01 · Push, Flow, Squeeze
01

LED

· the greedy one-way light

Use when: status lights: power, listening, error.

Rules: long leg = anode = + · always with a resistor · 5–20 mA · red ≈ 2 V, blue/white ≈ 3 V. [SparkFun]

→ lesson 01 · Push, Flow, Squeeze
02

Multimeter

· your x-ray glasses

Use when: checking any voltage, part value, connection or current. It is the first step of every debug.

Rules: black in COM · V across · A in series (red → mA/10A jack) · Ω and beep with power OFF · move red back to V when done.

→ lesson 02 · The Multimeter
03

Voltage divider

· two resistors, one fraction

Use when: scaling a signal down (5 V → 3.3 V), reading resistive sensors, measuring battery voltage. [SparkFun]

Rules: Vout = Vin × R2 ÷ (R1+R2) · 1–10 kΩ typical · never for powering a load · one-way only.

→ lesson 03 · Series, Parallel & Dividers
03

Series vs parallel

· the two ways to connect

Use when: working out totals before you measure.

Rules: series: same current, R adds, V shares · parallel: same V, currents add, R shrinks.

→ lesson 03 · Series, Parallel & Dividers
04

Power rating

· P = V × I

Use when: choosing resistors, regulators and anything that gets warm.

Rules: calculate the heat · choose a rating ≥ 2× it · hot to touch = redesign.

→ lesson 04 · Power, Heat & Batteries
04

Battery

· push + fuel tank

Use when: portable devices. Pick the voltage first, then the mAh for your runtime.

Rules: runtime ≈ mAh ÷ mA · Li-ion ≈ 3.7 V nominal · Li-ion needs a proper charger + protection. [SparkFun]

→ lesson 04 · Power, Heat & Batteries
05

Ceramic capacitor 100 nF

· the decoupling hero

Use when: next to every IC power pin, and in RC filters/timers.

Rules: no polarity · as close to the pin as possible · voltage rating well above supply. [Cadence]

→ lesson 05 · Capacitors: Tiny Buckets
05

Electrolytic capacitor

· the bulk bucket

Use when: smoothing the power input and regulator in/out (10–1000 µF).

Rules: polarised: stripe = − · voltage rating ≥ 2× supply is comfy · follow regulator datasheet values.

→ lesson 05 · Capacitors: Tiny Buckets
06

Diode (silicon / Schottky)

· the one-way door

Use when: reverse-polarity protection, flyback across coils, steering current.

Rules: stripe = cathode · silicon ≈ 0.7 V drop · Schottky ≈ 0.2–0.45 V · flyback goes ACROSS the coil. [SparkFun]

→ lesson 06 · Diodes: One-Way Doors
06

TVS / Zener

· the bouncers

Use when: TVS: ESD protection on connectors (USB). Zener: clamp to a fixed voltage.

Rules: recognise them in reference designs · place TVS right at the connector.

→ lesson 06 · Diodes: One-Way Doors
07

NPN transistor (BJT)

· the tiny tap

Use when: a pin needs to switch more current than it can give (buzzer, relay coil, a few LEDs).

Rules: low-side: load → C, E → GND · base resistor always · I_base ≈ I_load ÷ 10 · check pinout in datasheet · coils need a flyback diode. [SparkFun]

→ lesson 07 · Transistors: The Tiny Tap
08

N-channel MOSFET (logic-level)

· the big tap

Use when: switching or PWM-dimming big loads (motors, LED strips, fans) from 3.3/5 V pins.

Rules: RDS(on) rated at ≤ your pin voltage · 100 Ω–1 kΩ gate resistor · 10 kΩ gate pull-down · flyback diode on coils · AO3400A for 3.3 V. [Components101]

→ lesson 08 · MOSFETs: The Big Tap
09

Pull-up / pull-down resistor

· the spring on the door

Use when: every button, switch and open-drain line (I²C!) read by a pin.

Rules: ~10 kΩ external, or INPUT_PULLUP internal · pull-up + button to GND = active-low. [SparkFun]

→ lesson 09 · Buttons & Pull-ups
09

Push button

· momentary contact

Use when: user input: reset, boot, "talk", menu.

Rules: always with a pull-up/down · debounce (≈50 ms in code, or RC). [Arduino]

→ lesson 09 · Buttons & Pull-ups
10

Potentiometer

· adjustable divider

Use when: knobs: volume, brightness, thresholds. Trimmer pots for set-once calibration.

Rules: ends → VCC & GND · wiper → ADC pin · 10 kΩ typical.

→ lesson 10 · Sensors & Analog Inputs
10

Resistive sensor (LDR, thermistor)

· resistance that changes

Use when: cheap light/temperature/pressure sensing.

Rules: pair with a fixed R ≈ sensor mid-range · into an ADC pin · ESP32: use ADC1 pins with Wi-Fi. [Espressif]

→ lesson 10 · Sensors & Analog Inputs
11

Relay

· magnet-flipped switch

Use when: switching a separate/isolated low-voltage circuit; simple on/off of bigger loads.

Rules: drive coil with transistor · flyback diode across coil · modules include both · no mains in this course.

→ lesson 11 · Coils: Motors & Relays
11

DC motor

· spins when powered

Use when: fans, wheels, pumps, vibration.

Rules: own supply + shared GND · MOSFET + flyback diode · H-bridge driver to reverse · never from a pin. [SparkFun]

→ lesson 11 · Coils: Motors & Relays
12

LDO regulator

· the quiet valve

Use when: small drops at modest current, e.g. USB 5 V → 3.3 V, and quiet power for audio/ADC.

Rules: heat = (Vin − Vout) × I · check dropout (AMS1117 ≈ 1.1–1.3 V) · in/out caps from datasheet. [LCSC]

→ lesson 12 · Power Supplies: LDO vs Buck
12

Buck / boost converter

· the bucket brigade

Use when: big drops, big currents, battery boards (buck down, boost up).

Rules: efficient but noisy · follow the datasheet layout exactly · inductor + caps close to the chip.

→ lesson 12 · Power Supplies: LDO vs Buck
12

USB-C power (sink)

· 5.1 kΩ × 2

Use when: any board powered from a USB-C port.

Rules: 5.1 kΩ CC1→GND and CC2→GND, separate resistors · without them C-to-C chargers give 0 V. [DigiKey forum]

→ lesson 12 · Power Supplies: LDO vs Buck
13

Microcontroller module (ESP32-S3)

· the brain

Use when: any board that runs code and talks to your app over Wi-Fi/BLE/USB.

Rules: 3.3 V logic, not 5 V tolerant · decouple every power pin · keep the antenna area clear · copy the module datasheet reference circuit. [ESP32 forum]

→ lesson 13 · Microcontrollers
13

Level shifter

· translator between 5 V and 3.3 V

Use when: two-way or fast signals between 5 V and 3.3 V parts (I²C, SPI).

Rules: BSS138 boards for I²C · divider OK for one-way 5→3.3 V · check VIH for 3.3→5 V. [SparkFun]

→ lesson 13 · Microcontrollers
14

Bus picker

· UART · I²C · SPI · I²S

Use when: deciding how a chip connects to your microcontroller.

Rules: UART: TX↔RX crossed, same baud · I²C: SDA/SCL + 4.7 kΩ pull-ups, unique addresses · SPI: SCK/MOSI/MISO + CS per chip · I²S: BCLK/WS/SD for audio. [SparkFun]

→ lesson 14 · How Chips Talk: UART, I²C, SPI
15

Datasheet checklist

· five numbers

Use when: before adding ANY new part to a schematic.

Rules: supply range · current · VIH/VIL · pinout + package (check view) · typical application circuit · design inside recommended, never at abs max. [DigiKey]

→ lesson 15 · Reading a Datasheet
16

Schematic habits

· readable = reviewable

Use when: every board, before any layout.

Rules: power up, GND down, left→right flow · net labels · values on everything · decoupling caps next to their chip · ERC clean (PWR_FLAG where needed). [KiCad docs]

→ lesson 16 · Schematics in KiCad

Package picker

· THT vs SMD sizes

Use when: assigning footprints and choosing parts to buy.

Rules: 0805 for hand-solder passives · SOT-23 small FETs · SOT-223 regulators · THT for stressed connectors · check imperial vs metric · verify pin 1 vs datasheet. [Electronics Notes]

→ lesson 17 · Footprints & Packages

Layout rules

· the 2-layer starter set

Use when: every first board.

Rules: place before route · connectors on edges · decoupling hugging pins · GND plane on bottom · signals ~0.25 mm, power ≥ 0.5 mm (calculate) · antenna keep-out · DRC 0 errors. [LCSC calc]

Fab files

· what to send

Use when: every order.

Rules: bare board: Gerbers + drill · assembly: + BOM (with part numbers) + CPL · always view in a Gerber viewer · design within the fab's capabilities · review first. [JLCPCB]

Bring-up checklist

· first power on a new board

Use when: every new or repaired board.

Rules: inspect → VCC↔GND no beep → current-limited power → measure rails at pins → code one feature at a time. [SparkFun]

Voice board blocks
